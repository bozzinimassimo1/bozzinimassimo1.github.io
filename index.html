✨ What Is eido?

eido is a workspace for your computer that pairs you with an AI coding agent. Think of it as a desk with two chairs: one for you, and one for a helpful assistant that can read your files, suggest changes, run small tasks, and even look at what is happening on screen or in a browser window when you allow it.

It is built on top of two well-known open tools — Zed, a fast and modern code editor, and pi. You do not need to understand either of them to use eido. What matters is that the app feels quick, stays out of your way, and keeps the assistant's powers under your control at all times.

People use eido for a wide range of everyday tasks: tidying up a messy project folder, reviewing changes before they are saved, getting a second pair of eyes on a document full of technical text, or letting the agent walk through a website and report back what it found.

🖥️ Your First Five Minutes

Once the window is open, here is the gentlest possible path through the app.

Step one: choose a place to work

The app will ask you to open a folder. Pick a small folder you already know, such as a project you keep on your desktop. You can always point it at a different folder later, so there is no wrong choice here.

Step two: meet the assistant panel

You will see a side panel where you can type questions or requests in plain English. Try something friendly first, like "What files are in this folder?" Reading the answer is a good way to build confidence before asking for any changes.

Step three: ask for one small change

When you feel ready, ask for something small and reversible. The assistant will show you what it plans to do before anything is saved. Read the suggestion, and only accept it if it looks right to you.

Step four: use the review view

Review is the heart of eido. Any change the agent proposes can be inspected side by side with the original, so you always know exactly what would be different. Nothing is applied without your approval.

🔒 Global Agent Access Controls

The feature most people appreciate first is the control panel. From a single place you can decide what the agent is allowed to touch. You can permit reading, block writing, allow browser visits, or pause everything with one switch.

If you have ever worried about an automated helper doing something you did not intend, this is the answer. The controls are global, which means one setting covers the whole workspace instead of a dozen separate permissions buried in different menus. When in doubt, start with the most restrictive setting and open it up gradually as you become comfortable.

It also helps to keep regular backups of anything precious. That way, experimenting is always safe, because you can restore an earlier copy whenever you want.

    💡 Practical Tips for Everyday Use

    • Start small. Ask for one clear thing at a time rather than a long list. Short requests get clearer answers.
    • Describe the goal, not the method. Say what you want to end up with and let the assistant work out the steps.
    • Read before you accept. The review view exists so that you stay the person in charge. Skim every change, even tiny ones.
    • Keep the controls tight while learning. Turn features on one at a time so you always know what caused a particular result.
    • Use plain language. There is no special phrasing to memorise. Write the way you would explain the task to a colleague.
    • Close and reopen if something looks stuck. A simple restart clears most small hiccups without touching your files.

    🩺 If Something Goes Wrong

    Most problems have very ordinary causes. If the download does not start, check that your browser is not blocking it and try again from the official page. If the file will not open, give it a moment and try a second time — the first launch sometimes needs patience. If the app opens but feels empty, check that you have picked a folder with something in it.

    If a request produces an answer you did not expect, simply do not accept the change. Nothing is committed without your approval, so a strange suggestion costs you nothing more than a click to dismiss. Re-read your request and try again with slightly different wording.
